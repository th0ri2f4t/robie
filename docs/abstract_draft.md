In Nigeria, which is Africa's largest oil producer, ageing pipeline and process infrastructure suffers corrosion and third-party damage, resulting in continuous hydrocarbon losses through cracks and fugitive emissions. Leak detection relies primarily on manual surveys that expose personnel to hazardous zones, creating economic, safety, climate and regulatory burdens. This dissertation presents R.O.B.I.E (Robotic Observer for Boundary Inspection and Evaluation), an autonomous unmanned ground vehicle (UGV) which patrols oil-and-gas facilities to detect pipe defects and localise hydrocarbon gas leaks. The platform employs a four-motor mecanum drivetrain, a Raspberry Pi 5 mission computer, a 2D LiDAR scanner for SLAM and navigation, metal-oxide sensors (MQ-4 and MQ-6) with active sampling, and a Pi camera for crack detection. A layered safety architecture couples a software interlock to an ESP32 hardware failsafe that zeroes all motor PWM within one 500 ms heartbeat window of link loss or emergency stop (E-stop) actuation, while a forward-mounted pair of sharp GP2Y0A21YK0F infrared rangefinders provides reflexive obstacle avoidance.

The primary contribution, a gradient-climb gas localisation algorithm (state machine v3 with home-base pattern search, exponential-moving-average filtering and threshold hysteresis), is validated using a simulation-first, multi-fidelity methodology: a two-dimensional statistical plume simulator with calibrated sensor noise evaluated by ROC-style threshold analysis, and a three-dimensional Gazebo physics simulation validating SLAM Toolbox mapping and Nav2 autonomous patrol with obstacle avoidance. The mechanical design follows a parametric, flat-plate, kit-friendly philosophy to enable future educational re-packaging. Limitations, including encoder-less odometry, brushed-motor torque margins and sim-to-real transfer, are characterised and bounded.
